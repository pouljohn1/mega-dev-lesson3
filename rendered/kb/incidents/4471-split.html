What each one is

  • parser, fixed: the shared parser read every date month first, and every tenant on file writes the day first. Day and month swapped: 7 April became 4 July. It came in through the app, email and the booking-v2 form. Fixed by fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales.
  • hint, won’t fix: the booking-v3 form printed MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People at day-first tenants typed month first, as the hint asked, and the form read it day first. The swap looked the same as parser, but the parser fix did not reach it. Only form reports from tenants on booking-v3. The form is retired, so no new reports come this way.
  • shift, open: the date is not swapped. It moves by one day depending on where the invite is read (“Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.”). The desk listed these reports in its notes as a one-day move but filed them under parser. Neither the parser fix nor retiring the form touches it.

How they ended up in one ticket

Triage merges a report when its subject matches an open ticket (decision 0001). These subjects are generic (“Date is wrong”, “Month and day swapped?”), so every date report landed in #4471, whatever its cause. The intake tag does not separate them either: intake guesses from the reporter’s browser, and the cause depends on the tenant and the way in.

For the desk

  • A new report of a swapped day and month after 5 October: the parser fix did not hold for it. Open a new ticket, do not reopen #4471.
  • A report that moves a date by one day without swapping it belongs to shift (issue 3), whatever its subject.
